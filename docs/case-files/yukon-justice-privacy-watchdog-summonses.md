# Case file: Yukon Justice privacy-watchdog summonses

**Article slug:** `yukon-justice-privacy-watchdog-summonses`

**Case-file status:** Pre-publication

**Last primary-source check:** September 10, 2026

**Reporting hypothesis:** The Department of Justice used two court petitions to challenge five lawful IPC interview summonses, delaying an investigation into how it handled a request for correctional-centre video.
**Conclusion not established:** Justice litigated to conceal misconduct or acted in bad faith.

## Controlling record

The controlling merits decision is **Yukon (Government of) v Information and Privacy Commissioner, 2026 YKSC 50**, decided July 7, 2026 by Chief Justice Suzanne Duncan. The court dismissed both petitions. Use the judgment—not a party's characterization or news coverage—for the legal result.

## What Justice won or reasonably argued

- S2 found that the first petition survived the high strike threshold, had a reasonable chance of success, used an appropriate procedure and fell within the court's jurisdiction.
- S1 allowed the IPC to amend the records demand so it was addressed to the head of the public body rather than individual employees.
- S1 recommended that the IPC estimate interview lengths and consider whether all five witnesses remained necessary after hearing from some.
- The department's stated substantive position was that the WCC copy had expired, the remaining recording was controlled by the RCMP, employees had supplied everything relevant, and the original notice did not authorize the broader questions.

These points must appear in the article. They do not change the final disposition, but omitting them would overstate it.

## What the judgment did not find

- No final finding on whether Request 25-042 was processed contrary to ATIPPA.
- No finding that the requester was legally entitled to the 105 hours of video.
- No finding of bad faith, corruption, obstruction, retaliation or cover-up.
- No personal misconduct finding against any summoned employee.
- No adjudication of the IPC's conflict-of-interest position on departmental lawyers attending interviews.
- No verified total for either office's legal costs.
- No conclusion about why Justice selected litigation rather than another process.

## Right-of-reply questions

Send the Department of Justice the proposed central finding and links to S1, S4 and S5. Ask:

1. Why did Justice seek to set aside the summonses instead of allowing the interviews subject to specific privilege or relevance objections?
2. Who authorized the two petitions, and what public-interest or cost assessment supported that decision?
3. What internal and external legal costs did Justice incur in each court file?
4. Does Justice accept 2026 YKSC 50, and was any appeal or extension request filed?
5. Have the five employees since attended IPC interviews? If not, why not?
6. Why did Justice's position in Request 25-042 differ from the 2024 recommendation it accepted? What factual distinction does it rely on?
7. How did Justice access and collect 105 hours from the RCMP server if it considered the recordings outside its custody or control?
8. Was Request 25-042 reprocessed, and was any video disclosed?
9. What is the current retention and access procedure for APU video?
10. Does Justice dispute Pedlar's account that it required departmental lawyers at the interviews, his conflict characterization, or his description of costs and delay?

Ask the IPC:

1. Did the interviews and records production occur after July 7, 2026?
2. Is the own-motion investigation complete, and when will a public report be released?
3. What legal expenses were attributable specifically to the two petitions?
4. Does the IPC still require all five witnesses after the court's sequencing recommendation?
5. Was any appeal or further application served?
6. Can the office provide the formal notice and summonses, subject to its confidentiality duties?

Provide a specific response deadline and timezone. Invite both offices to identify factual errors and supply records. Do not represent these questions as sent until delivery actually occurs.

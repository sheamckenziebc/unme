## Intake and hypothesis

**Lead as received:** The editor supplied the CMQ's Waltham investigation report and summarized its findings: the director general placed his spouse on the payroll for almost a decade; cheques were the only documentary trace supporting ordinary municipal employment; later sworn statements lacked credibility; the director general proposed increasing his own salary after her departure; mayors signed blank cheques; and Waltham paid him for resources also used by his private business. The editor asked for the standard investigation workflow and publication once complete.

**Public-interest reason to investigate:** A municipality must be able to show who authorized an employee, what public work was performed and how spending was controlled. The historical findings are newly consequential because Portage-du-Fort hired the same former director general twice in 2026, most recently to manage its municipal office.

**Testable hypothesis:** Official records may establish that Waltham could not substantiate the director general's spouse's municipal employment, that the arrangement formed part of a broader failure of authorization and financial controls, and that the public record does not show what due diligence or safeguards Portage used before its current office-management engagement.

**Defeat condition:** The hypothesis would be materially weakened by contemporaneous employment authorization and work records, a credible independent explanation for the sworn statements and payments, a later decision reversing the CMQ's findings, a published observer report establishing full corrective completion, or Portage records showing a documented background review and robust independent controls.

**Most serious conclusion not currently supported:** The records do not establish fraud, theft, a criminal offence, civil liability, a knowingly false statement by an elected official, that the spouse performed no task at all, a precise overpayment or public loss, that the 2023 and 2024 budget lines equal actual pay, new wrongdoing in Portage, or endorsement of Roy by the ministry or CMQ.

**Jurisdiction, affected community and responsible decision-makers:** Waltham and Portage-du-Fort, Quebec; residents and taxpayers of both municipalities; Waltham's former director general and the prior councils whose weak oversight enabled the arrangements; Waltham's current mayor, council and administration for corrective disclosure; Portage's mayor and council for the current consulting engagements; and the CMQ for municipal-integrity oversight.

## Scope and novelty

**Questions this investigation will answer:**

1. What did the CMQ establish, and what legal conclusions did it expressly not make?
2. What evidence supported or failed to support the spouse's job?
3. Why did the CMQ reject the later sworn statements?
4. What do the public budgets corroborate, and what can they not prove?
5. How did blank cheques, weak records and private-business resources widen the control failure?
6. What is the strongest available account from Roy and his spouse?
7. Which corrective measures are visible, and which CMQ recommendations lack a public closure record?
8. What did Portage approve in June and August 2026, at what rate, and over whose objections?
9. What records would let residents evaluate Waltham's cleanup and Portage's current safeguards?

**Questions outside scope:**

1. Whether a criminal offence or civil cause of action occurred.
2. The spouse's identity or private life.
3. A purported total loss derived by multiplying budget figures.
4. Unverified motives of Roy, his spouse, signatory elected officials or either current council.
5. Whether private legal advice, police work or non-public observer activity exists.
6. A finding that Portage's hiring decision itself constitutes wrongdoing.

**Earlier UNME coverage checked:** The investigations collection and existing production case files were checked September 10, 2026. UNME has not previously covered Waltham, Portage-du-Fort or this CMQ report.

**Relevant outside reporting checked:** The Equity's August 19, 2024 report identifies Roy, publishes his detailed response and the former mayor's account; its October 9, 2024 report records the favourable presentation of Waltham's 2023 financial statement; and its September 8, 2026 report covers Portage's engagements, the mayor's rationale, an opposing councillor's concern, 97 attributed hours and Roy's current statement. **What is genuinely new:** The Dossier joins the 2024 watchdog findings to primary Waltham budgets and later control changes, bounds what remains publicly unresolved, and documents Portage's 2026 decisions, posted payable and current management assignment.

## Intake and hypothesis

**Lead as received:** After the June 24, 2024 Eagle Gold heap-leach failure, Yukon's Department of Environment continued withholding records after a formal Information and Privacy Commissioner investigation found that the department had not justified withholding all of the information and recommended disclosure. The lead said 14 of 33 pages remained withheld and connected the dispute to the scale of the failure, unfinished regulatory reforms and up to $220 million in territorial financing for the receiver.

**Public-interest reason to investigate:** The records concern the public response to a major mine failure near Mayo, on the Traditional Territory of the First Nation of Na-Cho Nyak Dun. The dispute tests whether Yukon's access regime can produce records after its independent watchdog rejects a department's withholding grounds, and whether the public descriptions of the withheld material are accurate.

**Testable hypothesis:** Primary records will establish that Environment rejected part of the IPC's disclosure recommendation after failing to justify specified exemptions, and that the public description of the remaining withholding does not accurately convey the response table.

**Defeat condition:** The thesis must be narrowed if the records were later disclosed, a court upheld the rejections, or the IPC supplies a documented method showing that “14 of 33 pages” is accurate. It must be abandoned if the response table or report has been superseded by a corrected operative record. S21 instead confirmed that the unit is sections, not pages; that resolves the count issue without defeating the core disclosure thesis.

**Most serious conclusion not currently supported:** The department illegally defied a binding disclosure order, concealed the cause of the mine failure, acted in bad faith, or caused a confirmed $220-million taxpayer loss. No reviewed authority made those findings.

**Jurisdiction, affected community and responsible decision-makers:** Yukon; Mayo and the First Nation of Na-Cho Nyak Dun's Traditional Territory; the Department of Environment as the public body that answered the access request; the IPC as independent adjudicator; the access applicant as the person entitled to seek court review; Energy, Mines and Resources and other regulators for the broader oversight context; and the court-appointed receiver for the confidentiality position attributed to it.

## Scope and novelty

**Questions this investigation will answer:**

1. What records did the access request cover, and what exemptions did Environment invoke?
2. What did the adjudicator find, recommend and expressly leave protected?
3. What did Environment accept and reject, and what reasons did it give?
4. What does “14 of 33 pages” mean when compared with the report and response tables? **Answered by S21: 14 of 33 redacted sections, not pages.**
5. What legal route existed after rejection, and is there public evidence of a court review or later disclosure?
6. Why does the dispute matter in the context of the Eagle Gold failure, current remediation financing and regulatory reform?

**Questions outside scope:**

1. Civil, regulatory or criminal liability for the heap-leach failure.
2. A technical re-investigation of the Independent Review Board's failure analysis.
3. Whether current remediation work is adequate or whether a prospective buyer should be approved.
4. The identity or motives of the redacted access applicant.
5. The contents of information that remains lawfully withheld.

**Earlier UNME coverage checked:** Repository investigation files and slugs were searched on September 10, 2026. No Eagle Gold investigation was found.

**Relevant outside reporting checked:** CBC Yukon reporting republished by Yahoo News on September 9, 2025 identified the requester as a CBC journalist and reported the IPC's “14 of 33 pages” description. It was treated as a lead and novelty check, not proof of the adjudicative result.

**What is genuinely new:** At publication, the public IPC summary and 2025 annual report called the rejected items 14 of 33 “pages.” Environment's attached decision table instead contains 33 recommendation lines, 14 marked not accepted. Those rejected lines identify eight unique record groups and touch at least 47 distinct numbered pages, sometimes only for specified redactions or passages. The public record did not then explain that mismatch. S21 later confirmed that the unit is redacted sections, validating the numerical audit while leaving the amount of withheld text unknown. A current-status pass also connects the unresolved public outcome with the government's 2026 implementation schedule, financing cap and unconfirmed independence initiative without treating those matters as proof of motive or continued withholding.

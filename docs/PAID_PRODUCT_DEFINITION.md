# Supporting subscription: product definition

**Decision date:** September 11, 2026

**Status:** Editorial direction adopted by the publisher; checkout, price and paid launch remain future work.

This replaces the earlier **The Follow-Up** proposal. Do not build or validate an exclusive Eagle Gold monitoring product, a paid briefing cadence or three sample member issues as a prerequisite to reader support. The source map and fictional specimen in `docs/paid-product/` are inactive research artifacts.

## The offer

> Support independent Yukon reporting that explains what happened, who it affects and what the evidence can prove.

Readers subscribe because they want this journalism to exist and continue. The reporting is the product; membership is a way to fund it. Findings, source links, corrections and substantive follow-ups remain public.

The initial audience is people who care about Yukon decisions and their consequences. No established audience, specialist network or demonstrated willingness to pay is assumed. The name and transparent method may attract attention; sustained trust must come from useful, readable, original reporting and fair follow-through.

## What support funds

- Direct reporting: preparing questions, pursuing answers and including people affected by the decisions.
- Finding and checking primary records, including reasonable records costs when approved.
- Clear Dossiers and concise Briefs about consequential Yukon issues.
- Returning to published work, correcting errors and showing what changed.

These describe the intended use of support. Do not claim a contribution has already funded a particular result or that a given amount purchases a fixed number of reporting hours without evidence.

## What a subscription promises

A recurring contribution, a receipt, transparent terms, straightforward cancellation and a reliable support contact. Any additional email delivery must be explicitly defined and optional before it is offered. No private findings, exclusive alerts, member-only investigations, guaranteed weekly revelations or influence over coverage are part of the offer.

A supporter may suggest a lead through the same editorial route as any reader. Payment buys no assignment rights, advance review, favourable treatment, confidential material, immunity from scrutiny or suppressed findings.

## Financial aim

The publisher's first-year target is **$200 a month**. Currency and whether this means gross receipts or money remaining after costs must be confirmed when setting up the offer; this document does not silently convert the answer into a net-income promise.

For scale only, a **CAD $5 monthly** option would need 40 paying supporters to produce CAD $200 in gross monthly receipts. Processor fees, refunds, service costs, tax obligations and labour would reduce the amount available. CAD $5 is an illustrative starting proposal, not an approved price. Recheck actual Canadian provider fees and calculate the net result before launch. Do not treat sign-ups, pledges or a 25-person pilot as collected recurring revenue.

The $200 aim is a modest contribution to sustainability, not evidence that the publication can yet pay for its labour. The case for support can be credible before the business is self-sustaining; neither outcome is established by a plan.

## Proposed public copy

**Heading:** Help keep Yukon reporting public.

**Body:** We investigate decisions that affect people in the Yukon, ask those responsible to explain them, and show the evidence behind what we publish. Your support helps us keep reporting, checking and following up. Everyone can read the work.

**Button, once a working checkout exists:** Support the reporting

**Independence line:** Support does not buy a say in our findings or protection from scrutiny.

Use the body as an explanation of the reporting model. Do not imply the two launch investigations already received fresh responses: their publication histories and subsequent outreach must stay accurate.

## Launch prerequisites

1. Confirm the price, currency, gross/net target and the publisher account through which payments will be accepted.
2. Choose the smallest suitable hosted recurring-payment service after verifying fees, cancellation, receipts, privacy, exports and Canadian requirements.
3. Publish accurate funding, privacy, refund and cancellation terms. Do not imply charitable tax receipts unless the actual status supports them.
4. Build one clear static support page and test the complete hosted payment and cancellation path. No backend, account system, CMS or editorial schema expansion is required unless a concrete need is demonstrated.
5. Verify the public copy against what the publication can deliver. Obtain the publisher's launch direction before activating payments or soliciting contributions.

Work on stronger journalism proceeds now. There is no artificial paid-benefit experiment to complete first and no authorization here to charge anyone or purchase a service.

## Learn from actual support

Track aggregate recurring receipts, fees, costs, cancellations and voluntary reader feedback. Ask what work readers found useful and why they support it. Avoid tracking pixels and individual reading surveillance. Expand coverage or spending in response to reporting needs and affordable capacity, without giving funders editorial control.

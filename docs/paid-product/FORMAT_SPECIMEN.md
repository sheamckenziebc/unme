# The Follow-Up: fictional format specimen

**Superseded September 11, 2026.** Inactive illustration of an abandoned paid-watch proposal. Do not produce or sell this format unless the publisher explicitly revives it. The operative offer is reader support for public Yukon journalism in `../PAID_PRODUCT_DEFINITION.md`.

**FORMAT DEMONSTRATION ONLY.** Project A, the documents, amounts, dates and events below are invented to show the reading experience. They refer to no real mine, company, government, person or jurisdiction. The fixture references are descriptions, not real citations. The former three-issue pilot requirement has been retired.

The example follows the same question across three issues: has a required report been delivered, and what does its delivery actually establish? A real issue may include up to three such changes, with working primary-source links and evidence states from the case file.

---

## Demonstration issue 1 — A cleanup allocation is not a cleanup result

**The Follow-Up · Project A · fictional Week 1**

**In 30 seconds**

- The fictional authority allocated $2 million for specified cleanup work.
- That allocation does not show how much has been spent or which work is finished.
- A progress report is due in Week 2. We will check for the report and evidence of completion separately.

**The question we are keeping open**

What work has actually been completed with the allocation?

**What the record says**

`CONFIRMED` in this fictional fixture: the allocation record authorizes up to $2 million. The separate fictional reporting order requires a progress report by the end of Week 2. Neither document records completed work.

**What would answer the question**

An itemized account of spending and evidence addressing the specified work. A report may establish that an account was filed while leaving its claims unverified.

**Next check**

The public reporting register after the Week 2 deadline. No reader action is required now.

**Fixture sources:** F1 — allocation decision, clause 2; F2 — reporting order, clause 4. In a real issue these would be dated, linked primary records.

## Demonstration issue 2 — The report arrived; completion is still unverified

**The Follow-Up · Project A · fictional Week 3**

**In 30 seconds**

- The required report was filed on time. That answers last issue's delivery question.
- Its author reports $600,000 spent and one completed work item.
- The reviewed records do not yet include independent confirmation of that item's completion.

**Since our last check**

The public register now contains the dated report. We have closed “Was the report filed on time?” The separate question about the work stays open.

**What changed our answer**

`CONFIRMED` in this fixture: F3 records timely filing. `ALLEGED`: the report's author says the work item is complete; this label describes the unverified assertion and implies no accusation. `UNRESOLVED`: whether the completion has been independently checked. The spending figure is the author's report, not an audited total.

**Next check**

Look for an inspection or another record addressing the same item. No inspection publication date has been established. We will check the register again next week; that is UNME's check date, not the authority's deadline.

**Fixture sources:** F2 — reporting requirement; F3 — dated register entry and progress report, table 1. No fictional claim of wrongdoing is made.

## Demonstration issue 3 — No new verification in the records checked

**The Follow-Up · Project A · fictional Week 4**

We rechecked the reporting and inspection registers. No new document in those sources changes last week's answer: the progress report was filed on time, and independent confirmation of the stated completion remains unresolved.

This does not establish that an inspection has not happened or that the work is unfinished. It establishes the limit of the public record we checked.

The original deadline is no longer pending; it was met. There is no verified public participation deadline to highlight this week.

**Next check:** Week 5. We will report either a substantiated change or the result of the completed source checks.

**Fictional check log:** reporting register and inspection index, both checked at the end of Week 4; both accessible; no new relevant entries. A real issue must link to its actual log and record any access failure.

## What this demonstrates

The value is remembering the previous answer, giving credit for a met obligation, keeping a different unresolved question precise and letting the reader move on with their week. No escalating outrage, fresh allegation or invented deadline is needed to create continuity.

For real pilot issues, replace every fixture with independently verified records and complete the reporting workflow. Never transfer these amounts, dates or circumstances onto Eagle Gold or another real subject.

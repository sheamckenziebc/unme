# Draft funding and editorial-independence policy

**Status:** Working decision memo. No funding programme or ad product is authorized by this document.

**Publisher decision, September 11, 2026:** The next revenue option is a supporting subscription for public Yukon journalism. Quality is the reason to support it; a separate paid watch, exclusive briefing or artificial benefit is not required. The price, provider and launch remain future decisions. `docs/PAID_PRODUCT_DEFINITION.md` and `docs/PAID_PRODUCT_HANDOFF.md` define this direction. Later revenue options below are possibilities, not current implementation instructions.

## Recommendation

Do not add programmatic display advertising now. At the publication’s current stage it would produce little revenue while adding clutter, behavioural-ad infrastructure and perceived incentives that conflict with the existing no-advertising, no-tracking presentation.

Advertising itself is not necessarily brand-damaging. A later, tightly controlled underwriting model could fit: a small amount of static, direct-sold recognition; no tracking; no auction network; no story-level sponsor; and a bright line between funding and editorial decisions. The cheapening effect usually comes from surveillance, volume, visual noise and disguised influence—not from acknowledging a compatible funder plainly.

## Revenue order

Develop revenue in this order:

1. **Reader support.** One-time and recurring contributions through a reputable external payment service, with no paywall on public-interest records work.
2. **Grants or fiscal sponsorship.** Seek journalism, access-to-information and local civic-reporting support with written editorial-independence terms.
3. **Syndication and licensing.** Keep public-interest local republication accessible while charging for commercial packages, print rights, custom graphics or commissioned translation when appropriate.
4. **Events and expertise.** Paid talks, document-literacy workshops or transparent research services that do not compromise active investigations.
5. **Ethical underwriting, later.** Introduce only after a public funding register, conflict review and sponsor policy are operating.

Affiliate links, native advertising, sponsored investigations, pay-for-coverage, data brokerage and paid removal or alteration of editorial work are incompatible with the publication.

## Independence rules

- Funders, donors, members and sponsors receive no right to assign, preview, suppress, edit or time editorial work.
- Newsroom staff make all reporting and publication decisions.
- A contribution never buys favourable coverage, access to confidential material or immunity from scrutiny.
- Material support for a specific project is disclosed on that project. Significant organizational support is listed in a public funding register.
- Editorial staff disclose relevant conflicts and recuse where independence cannot reasonably be protected.
- Anonymous support is accepted only under a written policy and never where the publication cannot assess a material conflict.
- A donor or sponsor may be investigated. When relevant, its support is disclosed prominently in the resulting work.

These principles align with the Institute for Nonprofit News standards requiring clear separation of advertising from news and transparent disclosure of material funders. See the [INN membership standards](https://inn.org/inn-network/membership-standards/).

## If underwriting is introduced

Use a product that looks and behaves like acknowledgement, not an ad network:

- one restrained text line or static logo in designated site-level locations;
- a flat fee based on term and placement, never clicks, profiles or reader behaviour;
- “Support provided by” or “Underwriter” labelling that cannot be mistaken for editorial copy;
- no native advertorial, pop-ups, autoplay, retargeting, tracking pixels or third-party ad scripts;
- no sponsorship of an investigation, topic vertical or series by an entity materially connected to that coverage;
- no sponsor message beside an article about the sponsor, its sector or a close competitor;
- written conflict screening, rejection authority and a public sponsor register; and
- contracts stating that support conveys no editorial influence or advance access.

ProPublica’s policy shows that advertising can coexist with investigative work when it is clearly separated and conflicted series sponsorship is excluded. See its [advertising policy](https://www.propublica.org/advertising).

## Decisions required before launch

1. Choose the legal and tax structure through which contributions will be accepted.
2. Obtain accounting advice on donations, sales tax, invoicing and restricted grants.
3. Set disclosure thresholds and rules for in-kind support, fiscal sponsors and anonymous contributions.
4. Publish a funding register and the final independence policy before soliciting organizational support.
5. Choose a privacy-respecting payment and mailing provider and document what reader data each receives.
6. Create a written conflict check for every grant, sponsor and substantial donor.
7. Define who may approve, reject and terminate funding arrangements.
8. Revisit underwriting only when audience scale, direct demand and operational capacity justify it.

## Near-term experiment

Start with a simple reader-support page that explains what contributions fund, who controls editorial decisions and how supporters are disclosed. Keep the site free of ad scripts. In parallel, offer the controlled excerpt and republication programme described in `docs/REPUBLISHING_POLICY_DRAFT.md` and record actual demand before setting commercial licence prices.

This memo is editorial and operational guidance, not legal, tax or accounting advice.

# Draft excerpt and republication policy

**Status:** Prepared for editorial and Canadian legal review. This is not yet a public licence or permission grant.

## Recommended decision

Keep copyright in UR NOT MAD ENOUGH work and offer a clear standing excerpt permission, with full republication and translation approved case by case. Do not apply a site-wide Creative Commons licence yet.

That combination makes accurate sharing easy while preserving control over translations, altered headlines, correction syncing, commercial syndication and material the publication does not own. Creative Commons licences are designed to be irrevocable once applied. A permissive licence could allow commercial adaptations, while NonCommercial and NoDerivatives terms can block legitimate newsroom uses or translations. See Creative Commons’ [licence overview](https://creativecommons.org/share-your-work/cclicenses/) and [chooser guidance](https://creativecommons.org/share-your-work/cc-licenses/).

## Proposed public policy copy

The text below can become a `/republish/` page after editorial and legal review.

### Copyright

Unless a page says otherwise, original text, graphics and data work published by UR NOT MAD ENOUGH are protected by copyright. We retain those rights so that edits, translations and corrections preserve the meaning and evidence of the work.

### Excerpts you may use without asking

You may quote up to 300 words from one UR NOT MAD ENOUGH investigation when all of these conditions are met:

- the excerpt is accurate and does not change the meaning or level of certainty;
- omissions are clearly marked and the excerpt is not assembled to create a misleading conclusion;
- the original author and UR NOT MAD ENOUGH are named prominently;
- the use includes a direct, working link to the canonical investigation;
- the use does not imply that we endorse a person, organization, product, campaign or conclusion;
- any correction or material update noted on the original is reflected promptly; and
- the excerpt is not placed in a paid advertisement, a sponsored message, an automated content feed, an AI-training corpus or a database offered for resale.

This permission applies only to material for which UR NOT MAD ENOUGH owns the necessary rights. It does not cover third-party photographs, videos, documents, maps, quotations, trademarks or other credited material. Obtain separate permission from the relevant rights holder where required.

Ordinary editorial pages may carry general advertising, but no advertisement or sponsor message may be sold specifically against the excerpt or presented as sponsoring or endorsing our work.

### Full republication and translation

Ask `editor@urnotmadenough.ca` with a subject beginning `Permission —` before republishing a full article, translating it, adapting a graphic, distributing it in print or syndicating it to another outlet. Tell us:

- the outlet, audience, format and planned publication date;
- whether the use is commercial, nonprofit or educational;
- what edits, headline, translation or artwork are proposed; and
- whether the page or issue will have a sponsor or advertising sold against it.

Approved full republication will normally require:

- the original byline and a prominent credit to UR NOT MAD ENOUGH;
- the original source links, methodology note and status language;
- a canonical link and a visible link to the original publication;
- no substantive edits without written approval;
- prompt correction syncing and a note identifying later changes;
- separate clearance for third-party material;
- no further syndication or sublicensing; and
- no implication of endorsement.

Public-interest local and community outlets may be licensed at no charge. Commercial syndication, custom research, print packages and commissioned translations may carry a fee agreed in writing. Permission for one use does not create standing permission for another.

### Fair dealing and links

Nothing in this policy limits rights available under Canadian law, including fair dealing, or the ordinary right to link to a public page. Canadian copyright can include both economic rights and moral rights, and news reporting is among the purposes for which fair dealing may apply when its legal conditions are met. See the Canadian Intellectual Property Office’s [copyright overview](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/copyright-learn-basics/copyright-learn-basics-protect-your-original-works-learn-why-copyright-matters).

### Corrections and permissions contact

For permissions, excerpts, print use or translations, write to `editor@urnotmadenough.ca` with a subject beginning `Permission —`. For factual corrections, use the publication’s corrections route. This is currently the publication’s sole inbox, so keep each matter in a separate, clearly labelled thread.

## Internal approval checklist

Before granting full republication:

1. Confirm UR NOT MAD ENOUGH owns the relevant text, graphics and images.
2. List every third-party item the republisher must omit or clear separately.
3. Approve the headline, excerpt, edits, translation and placement.
4. Require a canonical link for web copies and a visible original URL in print.
5. Decide whether the licence is free or paid and record the exact territory, format, term and publication date.
6. Name the person responsible for correction syncing.
7. Save the written permission and final published URL in the campaign rights log.

## Model considered

ProPublica’s republication terms are a useful newsroom model: they require credit and linking, limit edits, keep image rights separate, prohibit wholesale or automated copying and distinguish ordinary page advertising from ads sold specifically against a story. See [Steal Our Stories](https://www.propublica.org/steal-our-stories). The proposed language above is original and tailored to this publication; it should receive legal review before activation.

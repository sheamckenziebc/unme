_Editor’s note: This is fictional demonstration content. The organizations, records, figures and events described below do not refer to real people or institutions._

The first public record looked ordinary: a two-year construction contract with a maximum value of $2.1 million. The second record looked ordinary too. So did the third and fourth. Only when the entries were put beside one another did the shape of the transaction become clear.

Over eight months, three amendments increased the fictional North River Public Works Office contract to $4.8 million. The scope expanded from stabilization work at one site to related drainage and access work across three sites. No single quarterly disclosure showed the cumulative increase.

<KeyFinding
  number={1}
  title="The amendments added more value than the original award"
>
  The original ceiling was $2.1 million. Taken together, the three fictional
  amendments added $2.7 million—a 129 per cent increase.
</KeyFinding>

## What the records establish

The entries establish the dates, stated values and short descriptions of the award and its amendments. They do not, on their own, establish that the changes were improper.

<EvidenceBox
  title="Four records describe one changing contract"
  status="confirmed"
>
  Each disclosure carries the same fictional contract identifier. Read as a
  sequence, the records show the maximum value rising from $2.1 million to $4.8
  million.
</EvidenceBox>

| Record         | Date    | Maximum value | Stated change              |
| -------------- | ------- | ------------: | -------------------------- |
| Original award | Jan. 12 |  $2.1 million | Site stabilization         |
| Amendment 1    | Mar. 4  |  $2.6 million | Additional drainage        |
| Amendment 2    | May 19  |  $3.5 million | Second work site           |
| Amendment 3    | Aug. 2  |  $4.8 million | Access road and third site |

## What remains uncertain

The demonstration documents do not show who approved each amendment, what alternatives were considered, or whether a new competition would have produced a better price. Those are gaps in the record—not proof of wrongdoing.

<EvidenceBox
  title="The public file does not explain why the work was not re-tendered"
  status="unresolved"
>
  The quarterly entries give one-line descriptions but contain no procurement
  rationale. A complete investigation would seek the approval memoranda and
  evaluation records before reaching a conclusion.
</EvidenceBox>
